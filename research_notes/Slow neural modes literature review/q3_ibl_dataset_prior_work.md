**Inclusion criteria.** A source was included if it analyzes IBL Brain-Wide Map (BWM), IBL repeated-site (reproducible ephys) or IBL behavioural data, or is IBL-consortium work with IBL methods. It also had to touch at least one of: slow population modes, brain or internal state, engagement, arousal or pupil, time-on-task, nonstationarity or drift, low-dimensional or reduced-rank population structure, timescales, the block prior, or multi-region latent structure.

**Exclusion criteria.** Papers that only cite IBL without using its data were excluded. Examples are Burk and Averbeck 2026, which uses macaque data, and Reato et al. 2023 and Landemard et al. 2025, which use their own mouse data. Purely hardware or spike-sorting tool papers were excluded unless they bear on drift. Non-IBL datasets were excluded, but are noted where they bear directly on scoop risk.

**Screening counts (approximate).** About 245 Europe PMC records were screened, roughly 150 of them unique after removing duplicates. I also screened about 50 IBL publication-page entries, about 70 GitHub repositories, 9 Cosyne 2026 passages and about 60 web-search results. I examined about 45 items more closely and included about 32 below.

## Q1. IBL flagship papers (BWM Nature 2025; Findling et al. Nature 2025): do they analyze slow drift, engagement, arousal, time-in-session or dimensionality? What controls for slow confounds?

### Takeaway
Neither flagship paper analyzes slow population modes, time-in-session neural trends, engagement states, arousal or the dimensionality of population activity as objects of study. Both treat slow drift, attributed to Neuropixels probe motion, purely as a nuisance. They control for it with pseudo-session, synthetic-session or imposter-session nulls (Harris 2020) and within-block permutations. In peer review, the BWM authors explicitly called within-session factors such as satiation, fatigue and drift "interpretational and technical difficulties". They showed only a null correlation between reward rate and inter-trial-interval firing rate, so this space is open.

### Cited Findings
**BWM paper: International Brain Laboratory et al., "A brain-wide map of neural activity during complex behaviour", Nature 645:177–191 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09235-0. Preprint: bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.07.04.547681 (2023).**
- [P] The paper covers 139 mice in 12 labs, 699 Neuropixels insertions, 621,733 units of which 75,708 are well isolated, and 279 brain areas. The canonical analysis set is 201 regions with 62,857 neurons. — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] The analyses are decoding (regularized regression with bespoke nulls), single-cell condition-combined Mann–Whitney tests, trial-averaged population "trajectory" distances, a GLM encoding model with temporal kernels, and a basic Granger analysis between simultaneously recorded regions (Extended Data Fig. 4). — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] Population trajectories are trial-averaged PETHs pooled across sessions and mice per region, projected into three principal components for visualization only. There is no single-trial or slow-timescale dimensionality analysis. — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] Drift is acknowledged as a confound: "Spurious correlations can be induced in particular by slow continuous drift in the neurophysiological recordings, due to various factors including movement of the Neuropixels probes in the brain. Such slow drifts can create temporal correlations across trials." — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] The controls are pseudo-sessions from the task generative process (stimulus side), synthetic sessions from a fitted behavioural model (choice, feedback) and "imposter" sessions made by concatenating trials from other sessions (wheel). Nulls were repeated 200 times for discrete targets and 100 times for wheel targets. For block-related tests, a "within-block permutation test" was used, because random permutations "do not control for spurious correlations that can arise owing to autocorrelations." — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] Time-in-session behaviour is shown but not analysed neurally: "Towards the end of the sessions, performance decreased and first wheel-movement times increased" (Fig. 1e–f). Trials were filtered only by first-movement time between 0.08 and 2 s. No end-of-session or disengagement exclusion is described. — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] Arousal is mentioned only as a confound that was ruled out for choice coding ("nonspecific brain states such as arousal"). Pupil, paw, nose and licks from DeepLabCut, plus whisker-pad motion energy, are used as movement covariates, with a time-shifted null for movement tests. — [PMC12408349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408349/)
- [P] The objective block-prior analysis was **removed** from the BWM paper during review and moved entirely to the companion prior paper: "we have now removed the analysis of the objective prior from the Brainwide Map paper." — [Peer review file, MOESM3](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09235-0/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9235_MOESM3_ESM.pdf)
- [P] **Key peer-review exchange on within-session state.** A referee asked about neural correlates of individual and behavioural variability. The authors replied: "within-session differences are dominated by factors such as satiation/fatigue that pose interpretational and technical difficulties (for an instance of the latter, the effect of Neuropixels drift)." As a "simple test" they "correlated a sliding window of within-session reward rate with overall firing rate in all the regions concerned, finding no systematic correlation". This was rebuttal Fig. R1, using inter-trial-period firing rate against performance in chunks of 20 consecutive trials, per region and per recording. — [Peer review file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09235-0/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9235_MOESM3_ESM.pdf)
- [P] The authors also pointed to Ashwood et al. 2022 (GLM-HMM on the first 90 trials) and Bruijns et al. 2023 as the IBL behavioural-state work. They said "our behavioural training protocol is aimed at reducing individual differences in performance, which impedes a complete analysis of the relationship between neural activity ... and factors such as the reward rate." — [Peer review file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09235-0/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9235_MOESM3_ESM.pdf)
- [P] On referee requests for deeper analyses, including inter-areal communication: "Many of them are indeed under active investigation within IBL" and "Teams within IBL are engaged in many of those analyses (as partly evidenced by posters and preprints)". To enable such work, the release now flags which clusters were recorded on the same probe or session, and there is a table of paired recordings (`brainwidemap/meta/granger.csv`). — [Peer review file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09235-0/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9235_MOESM3_ESM.pdf)

**Prior paper: Findling, Hubert et al., International Brain Laboratory, Acerbi, ..., Pouget, "Brain-wide representations of prior information in mouse decision-making", Nature 645:192–200 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09226-1. Preprint: bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.07.04.547684.**
- [P] The paper decodes the Bayes-optimal block prior (main target) from inter-trial-interval activity in a window from 600 ms to 100 ms before stimulus onset (also 400 ms to 100 ms). It covers 242 regions, of which 73 (30.2%) were significant in electrophysiology and 58 (24.0%) after merging with widefield imaging. Widefield used 51 sessions and 32 regions. — [PMC12408363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408363)
- [P] Drift motivates the null: "both neural activity (for example, from slow drift in the recordings stemming from movements of the probes across trials) and the prior exhibit temporal correlations". In the Methods, "slow drift in the recordings can lead to spurious, yet significant, decoding of the prior", and "spurious correlations in Ephys data are likely induced by drift in the Neuropixels probes", whereas widefield "barely exhibit[s] any drift". — [PMC12408363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408363)
- [P] The pseudo-session method cites Harris 2020 ("Nonsense correlations in neuroscience", ref. 34). Block and stimulus sequences are resampled from the task generative process, with 1,000 pseudo-sessions per session for the main test and 200 for some secondary analyses. A result is significant if it exceeds the 95th percentile of the pseudo-scores. — [PMC12408363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408363)
- [P] Exclusions: trials with no response or a reaction time under 80 ms or over 2 s, and sessions with fewer than 250 included trials. **No control for time-in-session, engagement or session trend is described.** — [PMC12408363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408363)
- [P] An "embodied prior" control found the prior decodable from video or DeepLabCut features in 38.0% of sessions (65 of 171). Eye position was also tested. The authors conclude that the neural prior "cannot be fully explained by the motor features extracted from the video." **Pupil-linked arousal was not analysed as a confound.** — [PMC12408363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408363)
- [P] There is **no subspace or variance-share analysis** of where the prior sits relative to high-variance or predictable dimensions. Decoders are regularized linear models on region-wise spike counts. — [PMC12408363](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408363)

### Inferences
- The student's finding that a dominant slow mode tracks elapsed time is exactly the kind of signal the flagship papers chose to null out rather than characterise. Pseudo-sessions protect decoding of the prior from drift, but they do not tell you what the drift is (brain state or probe motion).
- The rebuttal's Fig. R1 shows that IBL looked briefly, at coarse resolution (20-trial chunks, overall firing rate, reward rate). It is a null and was never published in the main text. A rigorous test of brain state versus probe drift (using motion estimates, two-probe sessions, pupil and GLM-HMM engagement) is therefore not done in the flagships.
- Follow-up (b), that the prior is absent from high-variance or predictable subspaces, is not addressed by Findling et al. They establish decodability with nulls but never quantify the prior's variance share or how it aligns with dominant modes.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Supplementary Information PDFs of either Nature paper, only the main text, Methods and Extended Data captions via PMC. A supplementary analysis of drift magnitude per session may exist.
- The figure number of the BWM rebuttal Fig. R1 is confirmed, but its exact statistics (the fraction of recordings with significant correlation) could not be extracted from the text-only PDF.

## Q2. IBL reproducibility paper (eLife): anything on drift or nonstationarity?

### Takeaway
Drift appears only as a recording-quality-control criterion: cumulative absolute electrode motion estimated during spike sorting, plus visual inspection of raster displacements. Only one recording failed for drift. There is no analysis of within-session neural nonstationarity, state or time-on-task. Population analyses are trial-averaged PETH principal-component embeddings and lab or region decoding.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Citation: International Brain Laboratory, Banga K, Benson J, et al., "Reproducibility of in vivo electrophysiological measurements in mice", eLife 13: RP100840 (2024 reviewed preprint; version of record 2025), DOI 10.7554/eLife.100840. — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/100840); [PMC12068871](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068871)
- [P] Ten labs recorded 121 sessions at a repeated site (VISa/am, CA1, DG, LP and PO). 82 sessions were analysed and 5,312 single neurons passed the metrics. — [PMC12068871](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068871)
- [P] Drift QC: "Drift here refers to the relative movement of brain tissue and electrodes, measured as a function of depth and time ... Quantitatively, the drift is the cumulative absolute electrode motion estimated during spike sorting (μm)". The paper reports "we observed 1 recording fail due to drift." — [PMC12068871](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068871)
- [P] Population analysis: "Since PETHs are well approximated with just the first 2 PCs, subsequent analyses used the first 2 PCs only." Brain region but not lab identity was decodable from single-neuron features or responses. — [PMC12068871](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12068871)
- [A] Related IBL methods work on motion: Varol et al., "Decentralized motion inference and registration of Neuropixel data", ICASSP 2021, is listed on the IBL publications page. Windolf et al., "DREDge: robust motion correction for high-density extracellular recordings across species", Nature Methods 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41592-025-02614-5, handles "noisy, nonstationary" recordings. — [IBL publications](https://www.internationalbrainlab.com/publications); [Europe PMC DREDge record](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1038%2Fs41592-025-02614-5)

### Inferences
- The per-session probe-motion trace, the "electrode motion estimated during spike sorting", exists as a pipeline product. That makes the proposed test of brain state versus drift feasible, and nobody has published it as a neural-state control.

### Gaps
- I did not read the remaining 225k characters of the eLife full text. A supplementary drift metric distribution may exist, but no nonstationarity analysis was found in the sections read.
- I could not confirm whether the released BWM data use DREDge or an earlier IBL registration algorithm for the published motion estimates.

## Q3. Other papers and preprints (2023–2026) using IBL data that touch engagement, state, arousal or pupil, slow dynamics, timescales, dimensionality or RRR, foundation models, or multi-region structure

### Takeaway
No published or preprint paper found characterises a dominant slow, time-in-session population mode in IBL BWM data, or tests brain state against probe drift. The closest neighbours fall into four groups:
- **Behaviour-only engagement or state models from the IBL consortium:** Ashwood 2022, Mohammadi 2025, Cuturela 2024, Bruijns 2025 and 2026.
- **Between-animal cortical timescale versus disengaged-trait work:** Yin and Hiratani 2025.
- **Brain-wide timescale maps from the passive period:** Shi et al. 2025.
- **Multi-session reduced-rank and state-space decoders, and foundation models:** Zhang et al., Neuron 2025, plus NEDS and related models. These decoders model a "slowly-changing prior belief" across trials, but within-trial windows only.

On the prior, Liu, Schartner and Fiete (Dec 2025) model prior modulation as bias plus gain. Nobody tests whether the prior lies outside high-variance subspaces.

### Cited Findings
**Engagement and internal-state models (IBL behaviour; no neural data unless noted)**
- [A] Ashwood et al., Nature Neuroscience 2022, "Mice alternate between discrete strategies during perceptual decision-making". This is the GLM-HMM, fitted on IBL behaviour from the first 90 unbiased trials. — [IBL publications](https://www.internationalbrainlab.com/publications); [peer review file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09235-0/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9235_MOESM3_ESM.pdf)
- [A] Mohammadi, Ashwood, IBL, Pillow, "Identifying the factors governing internal state switches during nonstationary sensory decision-making", Nature Communications 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-66738-0 (preprint 2024). It fits a four-state input-driven GLM-HMM (two engaged and two disengaged, biased states) to IBL biased-block behaviour. "Greater past reward predicted transition to disengaged states, suggesting that disengagement may be associated with satiety." **Behaviour only.** — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1038%2Fs41467-025-66738-0)
- [A] Cuturela, IBL, Pillow, "Internal states emerge early during learning of a perceptual decision-making task", bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.11.30.626182. Engaged and biased states appear from the second training session. **Behaviour only.** — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1101%2F2024.11.30.626182)
- [A] Bruijns, IBL, ..., Dayan, "Infinite hidden Markov models can dissect the complexities of learning", Nature Neuroscience (2025/2026), DOI 10.1038/s41593-025-02130-x (preprint 10.1101/2023.12.22.573001). **Behaviour only.** — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1038%2Fs41593-025-02130-x)
- [A] Bruijns, Eckstein, Dayan, "Low dimensional latent structure underlying the choices of mice", bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.08.08.669381. It analyses more than 300,000 IBL choices from 139 mice and finds "notable effects of continual fluctuations in task engagement". **Behaviour only.** — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1101%2F2025.08.08.669381)
- [A] Ghani, Yang, IBL, "Active sensing during a visual perceptual decision-making task", iScience 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2026.115976. It reports wheel "wiggles"; "the performance benefit of wiggle behavior persisted after controlling for arousal state." **Behaviour or pupil only.** — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1016%2Fj.isci.2026.115976)

**State, slow dynamics and timescales with IBL neural data**
- [A] **Yin and Hiratani, "Cortical neural landscape captures mouse-to-mouse variability in anticipatory vs. inattentive decision making"**, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.07.11.664420 (v1 July 2025, v3 March 2026, unpublished). It uses IBL behaviour and neural data from about 100 mice and finds "large but low-dimensional differences in behavioral traits" ranging from anticipatory to "slower, more disengaged". "The characteristic timescale of population activity, measured during both inter-trial intervals and passive periods, correlated with an animal's anticipatory tendency across cortical areas, especially in medial visual areas." They frame this as "depth of underlying cortical states". **This is the closest state-and-population-dynamics paper on IBL data, but it works between animals rather than tracking state within a session, and it does not address drift or time-in-session.** — [bioRxiv API record](https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.1101/2025.07.11.664420); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1101%2F2025.07.11.664420)
- [P] Shi, Zeraati, IBL, Levina, Engel, "Brain-wide organization of intrinsic timescales at single-neuron resolution", bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.08.30.673281 (Aug 2025, preprint). It analyses 27,960 neurons from 115 mice in 223 areas, using autocorrelations of spontaneous activity from the 10-minute passive period at the end of each session (5 ms bins, fitted up to a 5 s lag). Midbrain and hindbrain median timescales are about five times longer than forebrain. Choice- and reward-selective neurons have longer timescales. **The paper does not discuss drift, nonstationarity, arousal, pupil or state, and describes no detrending.** — [PMC12407892](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12407892/)
- [A] An earlier version appeared as the SfN 2023 IBL abstract "Atlas of intrinsic timescales in the mouse brain". — [IBL publications](https://www.internationalbrainlab.com/publications)
- [A] Zang, Khanal, Förster, IBL, Churchland, Urai, "Age-related changes in behavioural and neural variability in a decision-making task", Nature Communications 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-74227-1 (preprint 10.1101/2025.08.22.671763). It uses IBL-protocol Neuropixels data from 149 mice aged 3 to 20 months (about 18,000 neurons in 16 regions). Pre-stimulus variability does not change with age, while post-stimulus variability quenching is weaker in old mice. **This is trial-to-trial variability, not slow modes.** — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1038%2Fs41467-026-74227-1); [S] [lab blog](https://anneurai.net/2026/08/20/does-the-ageing-brain-become-noisier/)
- [A] Smekal, Ladd, Zhang, Steinmetz, Linderman, "Time-Varying Background Rates in Neural Sequence Detection with GLM-PPSeq", Cosyne 2026 poster 2-195. It adds a GLM for "slowly varying fluctuations" in background rate to the PPSeq sequence model. "Applied to motor cortex recordings from the International Brain Laboratory, GLM-PPSeq substantially outperforms standard PPSeq in population activity estimation (R2 = 0.62 vs. -1.6)". **This treats slow background nonstationarity in IBL motor cortex as a nuisance, not as an object of study.** — [Cosyne 2026 program book](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6102ca347474c263c40150cd/t/69b2ee5acab23a50003b7f26/1773334106922/Cosyne2026_program_book.pdf)

**Reduced-rank, low-dimensional and dimensionality analyses of BWM**
- [P] **Zhang, Lyu, Hurwitz, Wang, Findling, Hubert, Pouget, IBL, Varol, Paninski, "Exploiting correlations across trials and behavioral sessions to improve neural decoding"**, Neuron 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2025.10.026 (preprint 10.1101/2024.09.14.613047).
  - It introduces a **multi-session reduced-rank regression decoder**, with session-specific neural patterns and shared temporal bases and a region-specific timescale factorisation. It also introduces multi-session state-space models across trials: an HMM for choice and an autoregressive model for the "slowly-changing prior belief".
  - It covers 433 sessions and 270 regions. Targets are choice, prior, wheel speed, whisker motion energy and **pupil diameter**, decoded from 2 s trial windows.
  - **There is no analysis of slow neural modes, drift or time-in-session.** The RRR is behaviour-supervised and within-trial. The state-space part runs on decoder outputs, not on neural latents.
  - Source: [bioRxiv PDF via PMC mirror](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/904381847f6cb873ec6bec4652840dd5/nihpp-2024.09.14.613047v1.PMC11419137.pdf); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1016%2Fj.neuron.2025.10.026)
- [A] Posani, Wang, Muscinelli, Paninski, Fusi, "Rarely categorical, highly separable representations along the cortical hierarchy", Nature (published 15 July 2026), DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10668-4. The preprint title was "Rarely categorical and highly separable: how neural representations change along the cortical hierarchy", bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.11.15.623878.
  - It analyses 14,000+ units in 43 cortical regions of BWM.
  - Within regions, selectivity is diverse, which "enables high-dimensional representations and therefore high separability".
  - **"Dimensionality" here means the separability of task conditions, not slow or ongoing population structure.**
  - Source: [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10668-4)
- [A] **Schartner, Liu, IBL, Fiete**, bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.07.30.667641. v1 (1 Aug 2025) was titled "Spatially distributed and regionally unbound cellular resolution brain-wide processing loops in mice". v2 (1 Jun 2026) was titled "Broadcast, structured, and sequence-dominated: brain-wide computation at cellular resolution in mice". The GitHub README now gives the title "Every brain region contains nearly every task response type".
  - It uses about 60,000 neurons, ordered by functional similarity with Rastermap and k-means on concatenated **trial-averaged** PETHs.
  - "One of the largest shares of brain-wide variance is explained not by task-related sensory responses ... but by neurons encoding internally generated, trial-timed broadcast sequences that carry contextual information and are biased toward hippocampal and entorhinal involvement."
  - **This works at trial-averaged, within-trial timescales and does not cover slow, across-trial or session modes.** The repository lists "the RRR fit for Fig. S2 and Fig. S4e" as an input.
  - Sources: [bioRxiv API record](https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.1101/2025.07.30.667641); [GitHub int-brain-lab/bwm_dmn](https://github.com/int-brain-lab/bwm_dmn)
- [A] **Liu, Schartner, IBL, Fiete, "How learned expectations shape brain-wide responses"**, bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.15.694430 (16 Dec 2025, preprint).
  - It separates sensory processors, choice or integration processors and movement generators. The prior modulates integrators and movement generators through "both initial activity bias and gain modulation", but not pure stimulus processors.
  - A few-parameter circuit model fitted to the population dynamics predicts psychometric and chronometric behaviour.
  - **It does not measure the prior's variance share or its subspace alignment. Slow drift, pseudo-sessions and engagement are not mentioned in the abstract.**
  - Sources: [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/search?query=10.64898%2F2025.12.15.694430); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.15.694430v1)

**Foundation models and ML benchmarks on IBL data (these do not analyse slow modes or state)**
- [A] Zhang et al., "Towards a 'universal translator' for neural dynamics at single-cell, single-spike resolution", NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv 2407.14668). ### Inferences
- **Follow-up (a)**, the brain-wide characterisation of slow modes and the test of state versus drift: no published or preprint IBL-data paper does this. The partial overlaps are:
  - Yin and Hiratani: between-animal timescale versus engagement trait.
  - Shi et al.: timescales of spontaneous activity up to 5 s lags, without drift handling.
  - Zhang et al. 2025: decoding of pupil diameter from within-trial activity.
  - The IBL in-house stillness and arousal pilot (Q4), which is the most serious overlap risk for any pupil or state component.
- **Follow-up (b)**, the prior not being carried in high-variance or predictable subspaces: no paper tests this. Findling et al. and Liu et al. show the prior is decodable and modulates bias and gain. Zhang et al. decode the prior with multi-session RRR (behaviour-supervised). Nobody relates the prior to unsupervised or predictive dominant subspaces. Note that Zhang et al.'s RRR is a decoder (neural to behaviour), whereas the student's lagged RRR is neural to future neural, so the methods are distinct.
- **Methodological precedent to cite:** IBL and Paninski-lab work routinely uses RRR on BWM (Zhang et al.; the RRR fit used in the Schartner manuscript). Reviewers will expect comparison to that work and to Harris 2020 pseudo-sessions.

## Q4. IBL working groups, conference abstracts, repositories and project listings suggesting ongoing work on brain-wide state, engagement or drift

### Takeaway
Yes, and this is the main scoop-risk signal. The public int-brain-lab GitHub shows active in-house work by Michael Schartner, an IBL staff scientist, in three places:
1. A **per-trial GLM-HMM engagement score for every BWM trial** (repository `engage`, 2025, with a public parquet file).
2. An October–November 2026 **pilot on brain-wide stillness, pupil and arousal and "default mode network" signatures** in BWM inter-trial intervals and the passive spontaneous period. It applies the Landemard et al. arousal model and includes explicit slow-drift controls.
3. The Schartner, Liu and Fiete BWM manuscript, which is being revised.

None of these targets elapsed-time slow modes or a probe-drift-versus-state decomposition. The engagement and pupil-state components of follow-up (a) do, however, overlap with active IBL work.

### Inferences
- **Highest overlap risk:** a "pupil / engagement / brain-state" component. IBL (Schartner) has already computed per-trial GLM-HMM engagement for all BWM trials and is actively analysing pupil-gated stillness and arousal populations brain-wide. Any student follow-up should cite and use, or differentiate from, `engage` and the stillness and arousal pilot, and frame its novelty around slow-mode dynamics and the drift-versus-state test.
- **Low current overlap:** a dominant time-in-session slow mode, lagged and predictive RRR at 0.5 to 30 s horizons, the probe-motion-versus-state decomposition with two-probe sessions, and the absence of the prior from predictive subspaces. I found no evidence anyone is doing these, though IBL said in 2024–2025 rebuttals that "many" analyses are "under active investigation".
- Because the IBL in-house state work is visible only through GitHub (no preprint yet), the scoop timeline is uncertain. A preprint on stillness, arousal or "default mode" BWM signatures could appear in 2027.
